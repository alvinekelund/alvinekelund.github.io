Vera

A voice companion for an older parent living alone.

The parent touches the tablet and talks. Vera answers out loud, in English or Swedish, about the day, the medicines and the people in their life, and tells the family when something is wrong.

What Vera does

Answers out loud

"What day is it?" "Have I taken my pills?" "Who is Anna?" One spoken answer with large subtitles and at most one picture card.

Prompts each dose

A spoken reminder at medicine time, repeated once. A missed dose emails the family.

Hears an emergency

"I fell", "I can't breathe", "there's smoke": Vera says the emergency number, shows who to call, and the family is emailed within minutes, quoting the words.

Says no to scams

Requests for money, card numbers or codes get "give nothing", the caregiver's card, and an alert to the family.

Never gives medicine advice

Vera never tells anyone to change, skip or catch up a dose. That stays with the family and the doctor.

A page for the family

The family sets up people, medicines, a shared calendar and the emergency number, and sees each alert.

Built for the person using it

  • The whole screen is the button: "Touch anywhere and talk to me". No menus, no icons to learn.
  • The day, time and date are always on screen.
  • Vera is a soft, round character, clearly not a person, who looks at the one card she is talking about.
  • Everything returns to a calm resting screen on its own.

Privacy

Each household's data is separate. What the family sees is shared only with the parent's consent, the family can download or delete everything from their page, and old records are purged automatically.

Where it stands

Vera runs end to end in the browser today against a real test backend, with 214 automated tests, including 39 safety scenarios. It is not yet in use by a household: hosting and the first family pilots come next.
